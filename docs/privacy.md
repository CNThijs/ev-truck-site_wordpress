## Data subject requests (access, copy, erasure)

1. Verify the person controls the email address (reply from `privacy@chargenet.energy` to that address and ask them to confirm). Deadline: one month.
2. **Access / copy:** WordPress admin → Tools → Export Personal Data → enter the email → send. It includes form submissions (items 1–3) with the stored consent sentence.
3. **Erasure:** Tools → Erase Personal Data → enter the email → send. Removes submissions (items 1–3) and keeps only the anonymous use count of report codes.
4. **By hand, because the tools do not reach them:** emails in the Microsoft 365 mailboxes (`info@`, and the BCC copies) — search the address and delete; backups (they expire by themselves after 30 copies; do not restore old backups without re-applying erasures); Google Analytics data cannot be tied to an email address, so there is nothing to look up there.
5. Consent-log rows (item 5) hold no identifier that can be tied to a person, so they are neither exported nor erased individually. **LEGAL:** confirm that is acceptable.
6. Write down the date, request type and what was done (for your own records; nothing is stored on the site).

## Flags that need your decision (LEGAL)

1. **Newsletter unsubscribe.** The site stores sign-ups but cannot send a newsletter or offer an unsubscribe link, while the policy promises "until you unsubscribe". Decide which tool sends the newsletter; then either unsubscribe happens there and the list is synced, or I add an "unsubscribe by email" erase link.
2. **Google:** the policy now says consent is the basis, that data may go to the USA and that the transfer rules in section 8 apply. Confirm the actual mechanism in your Google contract (EU–US Data Privacy Framework or standard contractual clauses), that the GA4 property keeps data at most **14 months** (Admin → Data settings → Data retention; set it, because the policy states the maximum), and IP/Google signals settings.
3. **Consent record:** the policy gives the reason "to be able to demonstrate your consent, which the GDPR requires of us". Whether the legal ground is a legal obligation or a legitimate interest is a legal call; the text does not name an article.
4. **Hosting:** STRATO is named. Confirm the data processing agreement is in place and add the retention period of their server logs to the policy.
5. **`cn_consent_id`** is listed as a functional cookie that needs no consent (it only proves your choice). If you disagree it must move behind consent, which defeats its purpose.
6. **Turnstile:** not in the policy because it is not active. If you switch it on it needs a row (Cloudflare, IP address and browser signals, security purpose) and a decision on its consent category.
7. **Retention periods:** 12 months for form data and 36 months for the consent record are code defaults chosen for this project, not legal advice (`retention_months` setting, `CHARGENET_CONSENT_MONTHS` constant).
8. **App text:** the policy still describes the ChargeNet app (login, license plates, payments). That text comes from the old site and is untouched.
9. **Dutch policy:** the Dutch text is a translation I wrote for the new rows (u/uw); it needs your read, listed in `docs/translation-review.md`.
