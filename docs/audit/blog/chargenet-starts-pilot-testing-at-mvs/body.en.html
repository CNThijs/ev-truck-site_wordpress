Connecting the Future of Zero Emission Logistics

Electric heavy transport is gaining momentum. Yet many transport companies still struggle to find affordable and reliable charging capacity for trucks and electric heavy machinery. ChargeNet is investing in collaboration together with partners to change that. In the project Connect & Charge, co-funded by the European Union, we test how private charging infrastructure can be shared in a simple and scalable way via a peer-to-peer platform.

The project takes place at Millenaar en van Schaik, a logistics company that already invested in a large EV charging location and locally generated renewable energy. A significant part of their charging capacity remains unused during the day.

ChargeNet opens this capacity to other logistic companies via a platform that matches supply and demand. This reduces infrastructure costs, increases utilization of existing charging infrstructure and helps companies that face netcongestion.

First test results

In the first technical tests, several trucks from Millenaar en van Schaik were connected to the private chargers. After authenticating with the ChargeNet platform through the ChargeNet driver app each vehicle started a successful charging session. These testing days gave the team a broader technical and operational understanding of the day to day challenges that Millenaar en van Schaik encounters, such as different chargepoint configurations, fluctuating charging profiles and the practical implications of working with heavy duty machinery. The outcome strengthens the foundation for the upcoming pilotfase.

Project goal

The project aims to show that private charging infrastructure can be shared safely, efficiently and affordable, so that more logistic companies can switch to emission-free transport on time. Through a structured pilot, we test when charging infratructure can be shared without disturbing internal operations, how planning and smart charging systems can reduce peaks, and how solar energy can be used optimally.

Expected project outcomes

The pilot is expected to deliver concrete insights into charging profiles, peak reduction and the utilization rate of shared charging infrastructure. It also provides a validated model for collaboration between providers of charging infrastruture and logistics companies with a need to charge their electric trucks. The outcome will be shared with stakeholders in the region, so other industrial areas can apply the learnings and accelerate the transition to zero emission transport.

Connect & Charge shows how smart use of existing resources can speed up the shift toward a cleaner logistics sector. Co-funding from the European Union supports the development of this innovative approach and helps build a scalable solution for future heavy duty charging.
